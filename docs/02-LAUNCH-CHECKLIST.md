# Business launch checklist – perfume business in Ghana

A practical order of work for a beginner with a small budget. Tick items off. Anything marked **VERIFY** is a rule, fee or price that changes – confirm it with the official source before you rely on it. This guide does not quote prices because they change; use the budget template (file 03) to fill in real quotes.

---
## Phase 1 – Foundations (before spending on stock)

- [ ] **Decide your business model and range.** Original designer fragrances, affordable everyday perfumes, oils/body sprays, gift sets? Start narrow.
- [ ] **Choose and check your business name.** **VERIFY** with the Registrar-General's Department how to register a sole proprietorship or company, and check the name is available. Also check the name is free on Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and as a `.com`.
- [ ] **Register the business** (sole proprietor, partnership or limited company – ask the Registrar-General's Department or an accountant which suits you).
- [ ] **Tax registration.** **VERIFY** with the Ghana Revenue Authority (GRA) what you must register for (TIN, VAT thresholds, income tax filings) for your turnover.
- [ ] **Permits.** **VERIFY** with your local assembly (MMDA) whether you need a business operating permit for the place you operate from.
- [ ] **Cosmetics/fragrance rules.** **VERIFY** with the Food and Drugs Authority (FDA Ghana) whether the perfumes and body products you plan to sell need product registration or notification, and any labelling rules. Imported goods also involve customs; ask your clearing agent or the Ghana Revenue Authority (Customs).
- [ ] **Data protection.** **VERIFY** with the Data Protection Commission whether you must register as a data controller. The shop's privacy page is only a draft – have it reviewed.
- [ ] **Open a business Mobile Money / bank account** so business money is separate from personal money. Paystack will ask for business and bank/MoMo details when you verify.

## Phase 2 – Suppliers and authenticity

- [ ] **Shortlist suppliers**: authorised distributors, established wholesalers, and trusted importers. Ask each: Are you an authorised distributor for these brands? Can you show a letter/licence? Do you give invoices and batch codes? What is the return policy for faulty/wrong items?
- [ ] **Verify before you buy big.** Order a small test lot. Check packaging quality, batch codes (many brands have official batch-code checkers), spelling, box seals, cellophane, bottle weight and sprayer quality. Compare against the brand's official website. When in doubt ask the brand.
- [ ] **Keep evidence**: supplier invoices, authorised-distributor letters, batch numbers. Record them under the supplier in Admin → Suppliers ("Authenticity evidence").
- [ ] **Be honest in marketing.** Only write "authentic/original" where your paperwork supports it. A counterfeit sale can mean refunds, lost reputation and legal trouble. If you sell "inspired by" or decants, say so clearly and never use a brand's logo or name in a way that suggests it is the real product.
- [ ] **Decants/samples:** only if you can do it hygienically, label them honestly (name, size, date) and the brand/supplier terms and local rules allow it. **VERIFY** first.
- [ ] Record each supplier's contact, terms and payment arrangements.

## Phase 3 – Money planning

- [ ] **Fill in the startup budget** (file 03). Separate **essential** from **optional** costs. Get real quotes for each line.
- [ ] **Keep an emergency reserve** – do not put every cedi into stock.
- [ ] **Price sustainably.** For every size: cost price + packaging + delivery you absorb + payment fee + marketing share + a margin for returns/damage + your profit. The dashboard shows margin and markup per size (Reports → Price check). Check Paystack's current fee on your own account before setting prices.
- [ ] **Start with a small, manageable range** – e.g. 10–20 fast-moving fragrances in 2 sizes, not 100. Reorder winners.
- [ ] Decide your refund/return rules (fragrances are personal-care products; many shops accept returns only for unopened, sealed items or for items that arrived damaged/wrong).

## Phase 4 – Set up the shop

- [ ] Run the software (guide sections 2–6), create your owner account.
- [ ] Settings: name, logo, contact details, WhatsApp number, address, hours.
- [ ] Add products with **real photos** and verified details; costs; opening stock.
- [ ] **Delivery:** decide your zones and fees. Test-call two couriers/riders in each main area. Typical options: your own rider in town, a courier or bus-station dispatch for other regions, and pickup. Add them in Deliveries.
- [ ] **Packaging:** protective boxes/bubble wrap/tamper-evident tape, thank-you card, invoice/packing slip (printable from each order).
- [ ] Payments: Paystack account verified, test mode tested end to end, then live keys (guide §9–10).
- [ ] Edit and **approve** every policy page; get a lawyer to check privacy and terms.
- [ ] Domain bought, HTTPS working, deployed, backups scheduled (guide §12–14).
- [ ] Order a test parcel to yourself in another town to check the full delivery experience.

## Phase 5 – Photos and content

- [ ] **Photography:** a plain light background, natural window light, a tripod or steady phone, 3–5 angles per bottle, one lifestyle shot. Show the real bottle and box. Do not use another seller's or a brand's photos unless you have permission.
- [ ] Write short honest descriptions (notes from the brand's own page, size, concentration). Do not invent longevity claims.
- [ ] Publish the fragrance guides (already included) and add your own.

## Phase 6 – Social media and advertising

- [ ] Create business profiles (Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, WhatsApp Business with catalogue). Same name and logo everywhere; put the shop link in the bio.
- [ ] Post a mix: product photos, short "how to choose" videos, customer unboxings (with permission), delivery proof, behind the scenes.
- [ ] Use the WhatsApp buttons: customers can message you from each product page.
- [ ] Consider small test ad budgets only after you have photos and a working checkout. Track which ads lead to orders. Start tiny and double only what works.
- [ ] **Consent:** send marketing messages only to people who ticked the consent box (Admin → Customers has an export of exactly those people). Order updates are fine for everyone.
- [ ] Referral and first-order codes are built in (Admin → Promotions).

## Phase 7 – Soft launch (first 2–4 weeks)

- [ ] Launch to friends, family and your followers first with a **small stock**.
- [ ] Fulfil every order the same or next day and message customers on dispatch.
- [ ] Ask for honest feedback and photos; invite real customers to review (reviews are moderated; never write fake ones).
- [ ] Fix whatever breaks. Keep a list.

## Phase 8 – Running routine

- [ ] **Daily:** process orders; check payments marked "needs attention"; reply to WhatsApp; record expenses and any cash received.
- [ ] **Weekly:** Reports → sales, gross profit, expenses, stock value; low-stock list → reorder; supplier balances; review complaints; backup copy to a safe place.
- [ ] **Reordering:** reorder best sellers when available stock falls to about your resupply time × weekly sales. Do not reorder items that have not sold in ~8 weeks without a plan (discount or bundle).
- [ ] **Complaints:** reply within the day, apologise if it is your fault, offer the remedy in your policy, record notes on the order, and refund from the order page when agreed.
- [ ] **Monthly:** compare profit with your budget; pay yourself a planned amount rather than taking cash ad hoc; set aside money for tax and restocking.

## Things this software does NOT do for you
* It does not register your business, file taxes or give legal advice.
* It does not verify whether a perfume is genuine – that is your supplier checks.
* It does not guarantee search rankings; it provides the technical basics (titles, sitemap, structured data).
